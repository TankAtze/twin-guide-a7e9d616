Make an explainer video with your own AI twin, for €0

This is how we made our PDM/PLM video. Every tool is free. You need a laptop, a phone, and about 30 free cloud GPU hours per week from Kaggle.

Before you start: consentClone only your own face and voice, or someone who agreed in writing. Tell your audience at the end that it was AI.

1Research: pick free tools

ToolAny LLM with web search: Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude. They are interchangeable here.Costfree tier

A broad question gives a long, generic list. Ours:

Research what tools you actually need to produce the video. Evaluate whether they have a free trial, student version, or free credits, so that we do not spend money.

The LLM does not know your limits. Learn the basics first, find your bottleneck, then ask about it. Ours was GPU power (laptops are too weak for video AI). This follow-up found Kaggle, which made everything else possible:

Is there a way to use some compute for the lip-sync models, maybe also a free tier?
Human checkSome "free" tools in the LLM's list were not free. Free tiers change every few months. Open each tool and check the limits yourself. In our case: HeyGen free = 3 videos × 1 min with a watermark; ElevenLabs free = no voice cloning.

2Script: write it for speaking

ToolLLM + your own fact checkLength~130-150 words per minute
Main learning: context in = quality outWhen the LLM wrote our script from scratch, even a detailed prompt needed round after round of fixes. Better: decide where you want to go, write a rough draft yourself, then let the LLM polish it.
  • Start with a real story (ours: the Airbus A380 cables that were too short, 2006).
  • Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Contractions ("didn't", "it's").
  • Copy the structure of YouTube explainers you like (hook, promise, chapters, recap). Never copy their footage.
  • Make a second "TTS version" for the voice AI: write numbers and acronyms as words ("A three-eighty", "two thousand six", "P-D-M").
Human checkEvery number needs a source. If sources disagree, say it vaguely ("billions").
Our mistakeWe built visuals before the script was final and had to redo several scenes. Lock the script first.

3Voice: clone yourself with Chatterbox

ToolChatterbox (open source, MIT) on Kaggle notebooksCostfree; Kaggle needs phone verification for GPUsInput15-30 s of your voice, clean, in the language and energy of the video
  1. Record 60-90 s with your phone (quiet room, phone 20-30 cm away). Speak like a YouTuber: awake, a bit faster than normal. Cut a clean 18 s piece.
  2. Kaggle: create a notebook, Settings → Accelerator → GPU T4. Upload the clip as a dataset.
  3. Run this (one sentence per call; 3 takes per sentence, keep the best):
!pip install -q chatterbox-tts==0.1.7 torchvision==0.21.0 "setuptools<81"
import torch, torchaudio
from chatterbox.tts import ChatterboxTTS
tts = ChatterboxTTS.from_pretrained(device="cuda")
REF = "/kaggle/input/my-voice/ref_18s.wav"
line = "In two thousand six, Airbus had a problem."
for take in range(3):
    torch.manual_seed(take)
    wav = tts.generate(line, audio_prompt_path=REF, exaggeration=0.5, cfg_weight=0.5)
    torchaudio.save(f"/kaggle/working/line01_t{take}.wav", wav, tts.sr)
  • exaggeration 0.5 / cfg_weight 0.5 sounded most natural for us. Higher exaggeration = more energy, but more odd words.
  • Join the takes with short pauses (0.3 s between sentences, 0.6 s between paragraphs) in any audio editor or ffmpeg.
  • Speed on a T4: about 1 min of audio per 1 min of GPU. On a laptop without NVIDIA GPU it was 7× slower than real time.
What failed for usThe pinned torchvision==0.21.0 is needed; without it Kaggle fails with "torchvision::nms does not exist". "A380" came out as "A3080" until we wrote "A three-eighty".
Human checkListen to every sentence. Our automatic check (speech-to-text) once picked a take that skipped "In 2006". Only listening caught it.

4Avatar: lip-sync your own video with LatentSync

ToolLatentSync 1.5 (ByteDance, open source) on Kaggle T4Inputa video of you talking calmly to the camera (any text), as long as the voice; 25 fps; plus the voice from step 3Speed~45 min of GPU per 1 min of video

Kaggle runs Python 3.13, LatentSync needs 3.10. So the notebook builds its own small environment first:

!git clone -q https://github.com/bytedance/LatentSync /kaggle/ls
%cd /kaggle/ls
!pip install -q uv && uv venv -q -p 3.10 /kaggle/env && uv pip install -q -p /kaggle/env/bin/python -r requirements.txt huggingface_hub
!/kaggle/env/bin/python -c "from huggingface_hub import hf_hub_download as d; [d('ByteDance/LatentSync-1.5', f, local_dir='checkpoints') for f in ['whisper/tiny.pt','latentsync_unet.pt']]"
!/kaggle/env/bin/python -m scripts.inference \
  --unet_config_path configs/unet/stage2.yaml --inference_ckpt_path checkpoints/latentsync_unet.pt \
  --inference_steps 20 --guidance_scale 1.5 --enable_deepcache \
  --video_path /kaggle/input/my-face/clip.mp4 --audio_path /kaggle/input/my-face/voice.wav \
  --video_out_path /kaggle/working/avatar.mp4
  • Longer than ~1 min? Cut video + voice into pieces at pauses and run one notebook per piece. Kaggle free allows only 2 GPU notebooks at the same time.
  • Pick calm footage: no hands near the face, no big head turns.
  • Show the avatar small (a facecam in the corner). Lip-sync errors are hard to see at that size.
Don'tMake a second HeyGen account for more free minutes or remove its watermark. Both break their terms.

5Visuals: animation as code + a free 3D model

ToolsHyperFrames (HTML/CSS → video, Apache 2.0), Blender (3D), Sketchfab (free models)
  • HyperFrames: npx hyperframes init, write each scene as an HTML page with GSAP animation, npx hyperframes render gives an MP4. A coding agent can write the scenes for you; describe one idea per scene and review every render.
  • 3D: Sketchfab → filter "Downloadable" → check the license → Download 3D Model → glTF. Import in Blender (File → Import → glTF).
  • Light background, thick dark text, min. 30 px at 1080p: it stays readable on a beamer.
Our lesson from feedback"If the blue and orange line is meant to be a cable … that's not understandable at all." Show the real object, big. One idea per scene.
Human check: licenseUse only CC0 or CC BY models (no "NonCommercial", no "NoAI"). Credit CC BY authors on screen. Ours: E-bike model: "electric cycle" by adeelafzal2015, CC BY 4.0, Sketchfab.

6Music + edit

ToolsHyperFrames master file + ffmpeg; music: Gemini "Create music" or Google Flow Music

AI music always wants to be epic. Say what you do not want. Our prompt v1 ("light, curious and optimistic, around 110 BPM") sounded like a product launch. v2 worked:

Calm, understated instrumental background music for a YouTube explainer video. Focused and thoughtful, not happy or euphoric. Around 90 BPM. Soft muted beat, warm pads, a simple repeating plucked or piano motif, deep soft bass. Low energy that stays the same from start to end: no build-up, no drop, no big melody. It should sit quietly in the background under a voice.
  • Get word times from your voice with Whisper (free, local) and start each animation on its word.
  • Put the avatar video on top, muted (the voice track is already in the timeline).
  • Sound effects: free sound sites need an account and a license check per sound. We generated ours with a short Python script (noise sweeps, beeps): no license needed.
  • Final mix: voice at about -16 LUFS, music ~20 dB lower and quieter while someone speaks.
ffmpeg -i master.mp4 -i music.wav -filter_complex "[1]volume=-20dB[m];[0:a][m]amix=inputs=2:duration=first:normalize=0" -c:v copy final.mp4
Human checkWatch the whole video with sound on the screen you will present on. Then do a blind test: show it to a few people and ask "Is this person real?"
